# Video concepts: Four Word Pass

For the PayPal AI Hackathon video. Keep this file out of the public repo, like `devpost-submission.md`.

## The rules we're working inside

- **Under 3:00**, uploaded to YouTube as **Public**. Aim for 2:40 to leave margin.
- **Show the real app working.** Every app screen is a real recording of fourwordpass.com on a laptop and a phone. AI footage and motion graphics frame the demo. They never stand in for it.
- **Problem, who, why** (the Presentation criterion), answered in the first 30 seconds.
- **Nothing borrowed.** Music licensed for this use, an original AI voice (not a clone of a real person or a famous narrator), and no PayPal or AG Grid logos in AI footage. Logos appear only where they're really on screen.
- **No fake stranger stories.** The long demo bills were written by AI seeding. The video needs a bill that real people wrote.

## The line everything builds to

> **Never spent. Only passed.**

A demo bill wrote this in its own eulogy, and it's the whole idea in four words. Every other payment ends when it's spent. This one keeps going and keeps something from every hand. Make it the last line of the video, the thumbnail and the end card.

## First: start a real bill this week

All four concepts need the same footage, and getting it also fills Open Books with real data for the judges.

1. **Recruit 10 to 12 people in different cities.** Different cities give you a real map and real passport stamps, because location comes from the request.
2. **Mint two or three hero bills** through the real checkout. The AI writes each bill's personality at mint, and that can't be predicted, so mint a few and cast the most charming one as the star. Screen-record the best mint on your laptop.
3. **Give each holder a three-line brief:**
   - Screen-record your phone from opening the email to passing the buck.
   - Record a 3-second selfie video reading your four words out loud, in portrait, somewhere that looks like where you live.
   - Pass it within a day. (If you sit on it, you'll get nagged, and that's footage too.)
4. **Fork one bill** partway through, so the family tree has a branch.
5. **Let a backup bill stall** to collect real nag emails. The creator writes the hero bill's finale on camera.
6. **Save everything the bill writes**: diary, nags, stamps, eulogy. That's the narrator's script.

Rough schedule: relay Oct 16 to Nov 1, AI shots and voice Nov 1 to 5, edit Nov 5 to 8, upload by Nov 9. The deadline is Nov 12 at 12:00 pm PT.

## Four concepts

### 1. Never Spent (recommended)

**The bill narrates its own life, from mint to finale, in the voice the AI gave it.**

The narrator is the hero bill. Its lines come from its real diary entries and nags, voiced by an AI voice cast to match its generated `voice` field (for example, Wendell's is "deadpan, nautical, secretly sentimental"). A few short lines written by us hold the diary together. So the AI feature narrates the video, and the judges hear it working before they see the screens.

- **Look: the world inside the banknote.** All AI footage lives in the bill's engraved-ink style: cream paper, green intaglio linework, crosshatching. That style hides the shot-to-shot drift AI video still has, and it matches the passport stamps, which are already ink illustrations.
- **Signature transition: stamp to world.** A passport stamp thunks down, the camera pushes into its ink illustration, and the illustration comes alive as that city.
- **Why it wins:** each judging criterion gets its own beat, the ending line carries the thesis, and the PayPal mechanic gets a dedicated moment (the landing).
- **Risks:** the voice has to be good, and the personality is random. Minting several and casting the best one handles both.

### 2. The Wild

**A nature documentary about the migration of the paper dollar.**

The product's vocabulary is already a nature documentary. Bills are released into *the wild* and claimed from it. They migrate (The Passage), call out when neglected (nags), reproduce (forks and the family tree), and die alone after 14 days (the eulogy). And researchers have tagged every one of them: that's Open Books.

- **Look:** AI "wildlife footage" of fictional banknotes. A lone bill in tall grass at dawn. A flock of bills crossing a coastline. Two bills circling each other before a fork. A hushed narrator in an original voice that doesn't imitate any real broadcaster.
- **Why it's tempting:** it's the funniest option and the one people will remember after watching dozens of screen recordings.
- **Risks:** the bit can eat the demo, and the problem statement is softer. It works only if 60 to 70% of the runtime is real screen footage inside the frame ("Researchers observe a holder adding four words").

### 3. Follow the Money

**A noir investigation into one missing dollar, solved with a public ledger.**

Somebody's dollar went missing. A corkboard with red string traces it, and that string is The Passage. The passport stamps are the evidence, and the diary is the witness statement. The break in the case is Open Books: "They kept the books. Open." Rainy streets nod to Wendell the Damp.

- **Look:** objects, hands, silhouettes, lamplight, rain on glass. No AI-generated actors' faces, which still look uncanny.
- **Why it's tempting:** it makes PayPal verification and Open Books the climax, which helps both Technological Implementation and the AG Grid sponsor prize.
- **Risks:** the story and the money get the spotlight, and the four-word game gets less of it. The tone is the furthest from the site's warmth.

### 4. Four Words

**Twelve people in twelve cities write one story, four words each, on camera.**

A pure relay. Each holder's 3-second selfie, cut hand to hand, while the bill slides out of one phone screen into the next. Every caption on screen is exactly four words. The bill's diary plays underneath as text.

- **Look:** real faces, real places, portrait video in a cream frame. Very little AI footage.
- **Why it's tempting:** it's the most honest proof of Potential Impact, it's the cheapest, and the real people sell it.
- **Risks:** it depends entirely on recruiting, and there's less spectacle.

**Recommendation:** make **Never Spent**, and use **Four Words** as its middle act (the relay montage). If you'd rather swing for funny, **The Wild** is the one to try. Its opening 20 seconds would make a good test.

## Look and sound

- **Palette** (from the brand): paper `#f4efe2`, ink `#17402f`, bill `#e3ead9`, serial red `#b3261e`.
- **Type** (from the site): Fraunces for titles, Inter for supers, IBM Plex Mono for serials, statuses and receipts.
- **Screen recordings:** a clean browser profile, real data, and the phone on Do Not Disturb except for the email. Record at 2x so motion graphics can zoom without going soft.
- **Voice:** an original AI voice cast to the bill's generated `voice` field. Keep it dry. The jokes land better under-played.
- **Music:** sparse and handmade: felt piano, pizzicato, a music box. It drops out completely for the landing and the last line.
- **Sound design:** paper, a till drawer, typewriter keys for the four words, a rubber stamp on every hop.

## AI shot list

Generate a still first in your image tool, then animate it with image-to-video. That keeps the look consistent. Use the banknote in `public/og-image.jpg` as the style reference, and reuse this prefix on every prompt:

> *Living banknote engraving: fine green intaglio linework on warm cream paper, crosshatched shading, subtle paper grain, gentle parallax camera move, quiet and tactile. Fictional currency only. No text, no lettering, no logos, no real banknotes, no faces in close-up.*

1. **The till.** A hand slides a folded fictional banknote across a café counter. It drops into a cash drawer, and the drawer slides shut into darkness.
2. **Hand to hand.** On a narrow engraved city street, a banknote lifts on the wind and is caught, then let go, by a series of hands down the block.
3. **Stamp to world (one per real city).** Start from a still that matches that city's real passport stamp. Push in slowly as the engraving comes alive: trams in Lisbon, rain in Toronto, steam in Chicago. Generate these after the relay, once you know the cities.
4. **Over the rooftops.** The bill rides the wind above engraved rooftops at dusk. Use it as a bridge between acts.
5. **The last page.** The bill drifts down onto an open book, whose engraved pages turn on their own.

Leave all lettering to the motion graphics. AI video can't spell, which is the same reason the passport stamps draw their lettering in SVG.
